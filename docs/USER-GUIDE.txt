RL CAREER CASTERS  v2.0.1
=========================

Two AI casters for your Rocket League / RL Career Hub matches: a male
play-by-play caster who calls the action and a female analyst who breaks
down goals on the replay. Both run on your own PC, so there's no account,
no internet needed after setup, and nothing to pay for.

New in 2.0.1: no more lost first words on Bluetooth.
- The casters now keep one audio stream open for the whole session, with an
  inaudible hiss between lines. Bluetooth headphones (and TV or monitor
  speakers) used to fall asleep in the pauses and swallow the first word of
  the next line; now they stay awake. If this player ever gives you trouble,
  set "audio": "basic" in config.json to go back to the old one.
- "Combine all 3 voice packs.bat": checks that your main pack, the 1.4 add-on
  and the 2.0 add-on are all next to the exe, then combines them into one.

New in 2.0: a new commentary engine, built to sound like a real broadcast.
- Broadcast pace. The caster talks most of the time, the way an RLCS
  broadcast does: who's on the ball, passes, clears, 50/50s, the kickoff,
  the pressure, then colour and stories (form, stats, momentum) when play
  calms down, and a breakdown over every goal replay.
- On time. Goals and saves cut straight in (whatever is playing fades out).
  A smaller call that can't start within a second is dropped instead of
  being said late. With the ball near a goal, only short lines start.
- Never three times. No line is said more than twice in a match. When there
  is no fresh line, a repeat is used rather than silence.
- Only true calls. The casters only call what Rocket League reports. The
  game doesn't say where the cars are or where a shot went, so there are no
  guessed "just wide!" or "on the break!" calls; hitting the post or bar is
  called from the game's own event. Stories stay true too: "Blue need a
  goal" is only said while Blue are behind.
- Tested against 76 real matches (sample replays, checked against what
  really happened): every goal called, about 95% of saves in close games,
  every goal, save, demo and woodwork call true, passes, 50/50s, pressure
  and kickoff winners called right 87-97% of the time, and no line ever
  said three times.
- New calls need new lines in your voice: record the 2.0 add-on with Cloud
  Studio 2.0 (about 4,850 lines). Until then the engine uses the lines you
  have, with the same timing and repeat rules; it's quieter in places,
  because most recorded lines are long.
- "Demo.bat" now plays the opening minutes of a real 3v3 match.
- The "chatter" setting: "normal" is broadcast pace; "chatty", "quiet" and
  "sparse" (big moments only) give more or less.

New in 1.1: real emotion. The voices now use Kyutai Pocket TTS, which copies
the delivery of a reference clip. Each caster has three: hype (goals, epic
saves, overtime, the final result), lively (saves, demos, big hits, the
clock) and calm (analysis and stats). Every line uses the one that fits.
Each recorded line is checked with a small speech recognizer and re-taken
automatically if words came out garbled.

New in 1.2: voice packs, the Madden way. Record every line ahead of time in a
free Google Colab "studio session" with Qwen3-TTS cloning real voices (yours,
or a friend who agreed), then the casters just play the recordings: studio
quality, instant, and no CPU load while you play. See VOICE PACKS below.

New in 1.3: the casters follow the match better.
- They fill the quiet: a line every 6 to 11 seconds when nothing is
  happening (quiet / chatty in config.json still scale this).
- A goal cuts in cleanly: the line that's playing fades out over a quarter
  of a second and the goal call starts. Saves and demos wait for the line
  that's playing to finish.
- The excitement fits the score. Big calls ("What a save!") are for close
  games, within two goals. Three or more apart, ordinary saves, shots and
  big hits aren't called; a really close save gets a plain call; a goal gets
  "Blue keep piling it on" or "One back, but it's too little, too late".
- Replays are just replays: hits, saves and demos shown in a goal replay
  aren't called again, and watching a saved replay is silent.
- They know a series. The same two teams playing again within 30 minutes
  is the next game: no "welcome to tonight's matchup" after game 1, just
  "Welcome back for game three. Night Owls lead the series."
- One caster: a voice pack recorded in one voice (the cloud studio's
  one_voice setting) plays as a single caster. Set "casters": "one" in
  config.json to do the same with an older pack.
- 285 new lines in the line bank, in modern RLCS style. Voice packs made
  before 1.3 still work; the new lines play once they're recorded (the
  cloud studio records them).

New in 1.4.2: a match recorder, for testing. Start the casters with
"Casters with match recorder.bat" instead of the exe and play as normal. Each
match is saved in Casters\captures ("match ....jsonl.gz", a few MB): what
Rocket League reported during the match and what the casters said, with the
timing of each. Nothing else on your PC is read or changed. The casters work
exactly as usual while it records.

New in 1.4.1: one voice pack again. Double-click "Combine voice packs.bat"
to merge your pack and its add-on zips into one zip ("... (combined ...).zip",
saved next to them). Where both have a line, the add-on's newer take is kept.
Nothing is changed or deleted: the casters switch to the combined pack and
ignore the old zips, so you can delete those (or keep them as a backup). It
takes a few seconds. Do it again whenever you get a new add-on.

New in 1.4: the casters know your season.
- RL Career Hub: if it's installed, the casters read your career save (only
  read; nothing in RL Career Hub is ever changed) and know the event, the
  stage, the series score, each team's form and the players' season totals.
  Game one opens with where you are ("It's the Open qualifier...", "This is
  the grand final!"), both teams and how they come in. Later games use the
  Hub's own series score, and say when it's match point or a decider. When
  the game only calls the teams Blue and Orange, the casters use the real
  club names from the Hub. Turn it off with "careerHub": "off" in config.json.
- Stat callbacks: "That's two goals on the night.", "Save number four of the
  night.", "That's goal number twenty this season." (season milestones),
  "Nobody in the league has scored more this season."
- A whole series without repeats: every line said in a series is
  remembered, even across restarts of the casters, and a line only comes
  back once the others have had their turn.
- Add-on voice packs: new lines recorded later come as a small extra zip
  ("... (add-on ...).zip"). Put it next to the full pack; both are used.
- Accents don't matter in names any more ("Fibérr" in a pack matches the
  game's "Fiberr").
The new calls need lines recorded in your voice (the cloud studio's
"record --addon"). Until then the casters just use the calls you have.

New in 1.3.4: calls that fit the play better.
- Shots get a short call ("Rw9 goes for goal!"), since most are saved within
  a second. The longer ones only come now and again, on a shot from far out
  that takes a while to get there.
- The big "how did that stay out?!" save calls are for the ball right on
  the line: the casters check where the keeper touched it. Other saves,
  even ones the game calls "epic", get the normal save calls. If a shot
  call is still playing when the save comes, it fades out for the save.
- A little more talk in quiet moments (a filler line every 5 to 9.5
  seconds instead of 6 to 11), and none while the ball is near a goal,
  so a save or goal doesn't land on top of small talk.

New in 1.3.3: voice packs load in under a second, even the first time.
Nothing is converted up front any more: each line is read from the zip and
prepared the moment it's first needed (a few hundredths of a second), then
kept for next time. Keep the voice pack zip next to the exe. Updating from
1.3.2? If it already imported your pack in full, that copy still works;
to get the disk space back (about 2 GB), delete the Casters\packs folder
and the pack is set up again in a second.

New in 1.3.2: the demo uses your voice pack's names. "Demo.bat" plays a
short scripted 3v3 match between the first two teams in your pack, with
their players, so you hear real name calls. "Demo - Falcons vs Karmine
Corp.bat" picks a different matchup; copy it and edit the names for others.

They call goals (with the scorer, assister and score), saves, epic saves,
demos, crossbar hits, big hits, the last minute, overtime and the final
result, and fill the quiet moments with analysis and stats from your match.


HOW TO USE
----------
1. Put "RL Career Casters.exe" anywhere you like (the RL Career Hub folder
   is a good spot). It makes a "Casters" folder next to itself.
   Updating from 1.0, 1.1 or 1.2? Just replace the exe. Your settings and
   imported voice packs stay.
2. Double-click it. A console window opens. Keep it open while you play.
3. Play a match. The casters start talking when the match starts.

It works alongside RL Career Hub and the Fan Replies add-on: it doesn't
touch the Hub at all. It listens to Rocket League's own match data.

Want to hear them first? Double-click "Demo.bat": the opening minutes of a
real 3v3 match (about 3 minutes, no Rocket League needed). With a voice pack, the
teams and players come from the pack. To choose them yourself:
  "RL Career Casters.exe" --demo --teams "Team A,Team B" --blue "p1,p2,p3" --orange "p4,p5,p6"
Names must be spelled as in the pack's players.txt / teams.txt (the part
before "="), or those lines fall back to general calls.


FIRST LAUNCH
------------
- Downloads the voice model (about 100 MB) and the line checker (about
  75 MB) once, into Casters\model. The old 1.0 Kokoro model is deleted.
- Records about 100 common lines in the background (a few minutes).
  The first match may be a little quieter while this finishes.
- At the start of each match it records lines with the players' names.
  These are saved, so players you face again are ready instantly.
- Turns on Rocket League's official match data (the "Stats API") by
  setting PacketSendRate=30 in TAGame\Config\DefaultStatsAPI.ini (or
  TAStatsAPI.ini) in the Rocket League folder, and in the copy under
  Documents\My Games\Rocket League if there is one. Originals are saved in
  Casters\backup. If the game is in Program Files, Windows asks for
  permission once: click Yes.
  >> If Rocket League was already open, close it and start it again once. <<

Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the exe isn't signed.
Click "More info" > "Run anyway".


SETTINGS  (Casters\config.json, edit with Notepad, then restart)
--------
volume            100        0-100 (also in the Windows volume mixer as
                             "Windows PowerShell")
chatter           "normal"   "normal" = broadcast pace, "chatty" = more,
                             "quiet" = big moments plus some colour,
                             "sparse" = big moments only
playByPlay        "male"     who calls the action: "male" or "female"
expressiveness    0.7        0.5 = steadier, 0.9 = wilder (and the odd
                             stumble)
checkLines        true       re-take lines that come out garbled
units             "kph"      or "mph" for shot speeds
casters           "auto"     "one" = a single caster doing both jobs (your
                             voice pack is one voice); "two"; "auto" = one
                             when the pack says it was recorded in one voice
pronunciations    {}         fix how a name is said, e.g.
                             { "Kaydop": "Kay dop", "TechTron": "Tech Tron" }
rocketLeagueDir   ""         only if the game isn't found automatically
                             (the folder with Binaries and TAGame in it)
ttsThreads        6          CPU threads for the voices
audio             "auto"     "auto" = one steady audio stream (keeps
                             Bluetooth headphones awake, so no line loses
                             its first word); "basic" = the old player


VOICE PACKS (best quality)
-----------
1. Record six clips, 8-15 seconds each, of the people who will be the casters
   (only people who agreed): pbp-hype, pbp-lively, pbp-calm, analyst-hype,
   analyst-lively, analyst-calm. Read the script in the notebook's step 2,
   with real energy for hype and a relaxed voice for calm.
2. Open "RL Career Casters Studio.ipynb" in Google Colab
   (colab.research.google.com > File > Upload notebook), choose
   Runtime > Change runtime type > T4 GPU, fill in step 2 (tick the consent
   box, list your players and teams), and run the steps in order.
3. Put the zip it gives you ("RL Career Casters Voice Pack - ....zip") next to
   RL Career Casters.exe and start the casters. They set it up on launch
   (under a second) and say "Using voice pack". Leave the zip there: the
   lines are read from it as they're needed.
- Players and teams that aren't in the pack get the casters' general lines
  ("What a save!" instead of "What a save from Vortex!").
- To add players later, run the notebook again with the same pack name; only
  the new lines are recorded. Replace the old zip with the new one.
- Add-on packs ("... (add-on ...).zip") go next to the pack and are used
  automatically. "Combine voice packs.bat" turns the pack and its add-ons
  into one zip. "Combine all 3 voice packs.bat" does the same, but first
  checks that the main pack, the 1.4 add-on and the 2.0 add-on are all there.
- voicePack in config.json: "auto" (use a pack if there is one) or "off"
  (always use live voices).

CHANGE THE LIVE VOICES (no voice pack)
--------------------------------------
The six clips in Casters\voices are the casters:
  male-hype.wav  male-lively.wav  male-calm.wav
  female-hype.wav  female-lively.wav  female-calm.wav
Replace any of them with your own 8-12 second WAV of someone talking the
way you want (only use a voice you have permission to use). The casters
re-record their lines with it. Delete the voices folder to get the
originals back.


GOOD TO KNOW
------------
- Commentary comes out of your default Windows audio device. If it's hard to
  hear, turn the in-game music down a little.
- Everything said is also written to Casters\casters.log.
- Record a session: "RL Career Casters.exe" --record match.wav


TROUBLESHOOTING
---------------
- "Waiting for Rocket League match data..." and nothing happens in a match:
  restart Rocket League once (it reads the match data setting at launch).
  Check Casters\casters.log for "Turned on Rocket League match data" or
  instructions to add it by hand.
- "Could not find Rocket League": set rocketLeagueDir in config.json.
- Lines start late or lose their first word: make sure "audio" is "auto" in
  config.json. If casters.log says "using the basic player", the steady
  stream couldn't start on this PC; the log line says why.
- No sound at all after updating: set "audio": "basic" in config.json and
  restart the casters.
- "Could not start the voices": install the Microsoft Visual C++
  Redistributable (x64) and start the casters again.
- No sound but lines appear in the window: check the volume mixer for
  "Windows PowerShell".
- Too much talking: set "chatter" to "quiet" (or "sparse").
- Without a voice pack (live voices), lines often late or skipped: lower
  "chatter" to "quiet", or raise ttsThreads if your CPU has more cores.
- "The casters are already running in another window" but none is open:
  Windows has reserved the port the casters use (49198). In an
  administrator cmd window run  net stop winnat  then  net start winnat
  and start the casters again.
- To re-record everything, delete the Casters\cache folder.


REMOVE IT
---------
Delete the exe and the Casters folder. To switch Rocket League's match data
back off, copy the file from Casters\backup back into the game's
TAGame\Config folder (or set PacketSendRate=0 there).
